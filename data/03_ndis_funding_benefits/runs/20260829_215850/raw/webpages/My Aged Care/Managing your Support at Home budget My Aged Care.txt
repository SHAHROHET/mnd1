When you receive Support at Home services, you are allocated funding to pay for the services you need. This is your Support at Home budget.

Your budget depends on your classification and includes contributions from you and the government.

This page covers how a Support at Home budget works, how to manage your funds, what to do with unspent funds, and more.

## How does my Support at Home budget work?

You will be allocated a funding amount based on your Support at Home classification.

For ongoing classifications, the funding is split into 4 budgets that each cover 3 months of the year. This is your quarterly budget.

For short-term classifications, the budget covers the specific period of the pathway or scheme:

- up to 16 weeks for the Restorative Care Pathway

- 12 weeks for the End-of-Life Pathway (or up to 16 weeks if funding is still available)

- 12 months for the AT-HM scheme (in most cases).

## The government is changing the End-of-Life Pathway

The government is changing the End-of-Life Pathway to provide a second round of funding for participants who live beyond the initial 12-week funding period. This will help ensure continuity of care for older people and their families at a critical time. This change will commence from early 2027.

### What makes up my budget?

Your Support at Home budget is made up of:

- funding from the Australian Government

- your contributions (where applicable).

See more

If you receive ongoing services, 10% of your quarterly budget will go towards care management.

Your contributions will be based on your income and assets, and they will vary by the amount and type of service you receive. This is a change from the income test and daily fee that was previously used for home care.

If you’re a full pensioner or part pensioner, your Support at Home contributions are determined by your existing age pension income and assets assessment .

If you are a non-pensioner and Services Australia does not already have your income and asset details, they will ask you to provide them. If you don’t give them this information, you will be charged the highest rates.

Services Australia releases Support at Home budgets for ongoing classifications at the start of each quarter (July, October, January, April). Your first quarterly budget will be calculated from the date you entered care to the last day of the quarter. For every quarter after that, you will get a budget for the full quarter.

### What if I need more funding than my budget allows?

If your situation changes and you need services over and above your allocated budget, you can request a reassessment. This will determine whether you can be approved for a higher classification level.

You can also enter into a private agreement with your provider to purchase extra services. These won’t be funded by the government.

### Can I get interim funding while I wait?

If there’s a delay in getting full funding through the Support at Home Priority System, you may get interim funding instead. This is 60% of your total funding, and it helps you get the most important services first, so you can keep living at home.

The rest of your budget (the remaining 40%) will be given to you when it's available. The time you wait to receive full funding will depend on the level of demand for Support at Home.

When you receive interim funding, you'll need to choose a provider and enter into a service agreement with them. Together, you will decide what approved services should be delivered within your interim budget.

When full funding becomes available, you will be allocated the remaining 40%. This amount will not be backdated.

Note that all participants classified as urgent (i.e. whose circumstances mean that they require urgent access to funding) will receive full funding within one month and do not have any time on interim funding. Participants who receive a place under the End of Life or Restorative Care pathways will be immediately assigned full funding for their place.

To learn more, read the Support at Home - The new priority system and funding allocation .

### Can I carry over unspent funds from my quarterly budget?

Yes, but only for ongoing services. Funding for the AT-HM scheme is valid for 12 months (in most cases). Funding for the Restorative Care Pathway and the End-of-Life Pathway is valid for the duration of the episode.

Your provider should help you make the most of your quarterly budget, but sometimes you might not be able to spend it all. Unspent funds can help cover unexpected needs or changes to your situation.

At the end of a quarter, unspent funds will automatically carry over to the next quarter. The rollover amount is whichever is higher:

- a capped amount of $1,000, or

- 10% of your quarterly budget (inclusive of supplements).

This means that the maximum amount available in a quarter is:

- your quarterly budget plus $1,000, or

- your quarterly budget, plus 10% carried over from your previous quarter’s budget.

Services Australia will calculate the rollover amount either 61 days after the quarter ends or the day after your provider sends in their final claim for the quarter – whichever is sooner.

Once the unspent fund amount is confirmed, it is added to your quarterly budget in your Support at Home account. It can be used to access services in accordance with your assessed needs.

### What happens to my unspent Home Care Package funds?

If you were receiving a Home Care Package (HCP) and had government unspent funds as of 31 October 2025, you will have carried them over to Support at Home.

They will be kept in a separate budget and can be used for assistive technology and home modifications or for approved Support at Home services if your quarterly budget runs out.

You do not need to do anything to use your government unspent HCP funds. These funds will be managed by Services Australia and your provider, and will transfer with you if you change providers.

You can ask your provider for information about your unspent HCP funds.

### Can I go over my quarterly funds?

If a provider gives you more services than your quarterly budget allows, that’s called an overspend. This is something that shouldn’t happen.

You and your care partner should work together to manage your budget. It’s the provider’s responsibility to make sure spending stays within the limit.

Your Support at Home account can’t go into a negative balance. Services Australia won’t pay providers for services if there isn’t enough funding in your budget.

If there isn’t enough funding to cover all claimed services, the extra services must either:

- be paid by you (if this was agreed in your service agreement), or

- be written off by your provider.

Providers must submit accurate claims that include all costs related to services delivered within the relevant period.
