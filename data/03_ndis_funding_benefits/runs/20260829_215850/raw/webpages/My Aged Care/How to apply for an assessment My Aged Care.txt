How to apply for an assessment

Apply for an assessment online

Preparing for your assessment

Applying for an assessment is the first step to accessing government-funded aged care.

It's easy to apply and takes 15 to 20 minutes to complete. Arranging for someone to be with you while you apply can help make the process even easier.

## Apply online, by phone or in person

There are 3 ways you can apply for an assessment.

- Online. It’s quick and easy to apply online, and all you need is your Medicare card. It takes 15–20 minutes to complete the application, so make sure you have a little spare time. With your permission, someone else can also apply on your behalf.

- Apply for an assessment online now .

- By phone. You can call if you’d like to speak with someone. Our staff can answer questions and complete your application over the phone. If you need help calling, please visit Contact us to see the support available, including interpreting services if you speak a language other than English. A friend or family member can join the call with you, if you want.

- Call My Aged Care on 1800 200 422 .

- In person. You can apply in person if you’d like to speak with someone face-to-face. To apply in person, you need to make an appointment with an Aged Care Specialist Officer (ACSO) at a Services Australia office (Monday to Friday, 8am to 5pm). If you want to talk with someone in your own language, mention this when you make an appointment.

- Make a booking to talk with an ASCO on 1800 227 475 .

## Ready to apply for an assessment?

Apply online now

## Can someone help me apply?

Yes, it’s always helpful to arrange to have a friend, family member or other supporter with you while you complete your application. You don’t have to be alone in this process.

Expand all

### Family, friends and carers

A family member, friend or carer can support you to apply online or over the phone. With your permission, they can also complete the application or talk on your behalf.

If you want someone to speak and act on your behalf on an ongoing basis, you can appoint them as your registered supporter with My Aged Care. This can be set up at the same time as you apply for an assessment, or the assessor can do this for you during your assessment. The person you want to register as your supporter will need to be there with you.

Find out how a registered supporter can help you .

### Accessibility, translation and interpreter services

If you speak another language or have a hearing impairment, there are services to assist you:

- National Relay Service : for those with hearing or speech impairments

- National Sign Language Program : provides free sign language interpreting and captioning services for Deaf, Deafblind and hard of hearing older people

- Translating and Interpreting Service (TIS) : help in languages other than English

- Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander language support : call 1800 200 422 and ask for an interpreter in your language.

For more information, visit our Contact us page.

### Advocacy and support services

You can also get help from an advocate through the Older Persons Advocacy Network (OPAN) . An advocate provides independent help that is in your best interest. With your consent, an advocate can also speak to My Aged Care on your behalf.

Read more about seeking support from an advocate .

If you’re an older Aboriginal and/or Torres Strait Islander person, the Elder Care Support program can help you, your family and carers, to guide you with your aged care services and choosing a culturally safe provider.

With your consent, an Elder Care support worker can attend your assessment with you to help, so you feel culturally safe with the process. If you choose, you can also have family support with you.

Read more about the Elder Care Support program .

If you don’t have family, friends, a carer or a representative you are comfortable receiving help from, Care finders may be able to help you.

Read more about getting help from a care finder .

## What will you ask me when I apply?

We’ll first ask you some simple questions to check if you meet the requirements for an assessment.

We’ll ask about your current situation and your goals; this includes:

- your health

- how you’re managing at home

- any support you’re currently receiving

- what could help you live well (e.g. to maintain a healthy lifestyle, do more of the things you enjoy or get around more easily).

Your answers will help us understand your needs and whether you are eligible for an assessment.

If you meet the requirements, we will ask you for a few more details to complete your application.

My Aged Care will keep a record of your application. This means you won’t have to provide the same information again at your assessment.

Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people can request an Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander assessment organisation, if available in their area.

Read more about Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander aged care assessment organisations .

## What happens after I apply?

After submitting your application, an assessor will contact you to arrange an assessment. Usually, you will be contacted within 2 to 6 weeks. An assessment can determine exactly what you need. It can even let you know about services you may not have thought about.

We’ll also send you a My Aged Care welcome pack in the mail. It has helpful information and outlines your next steps.

You can track your application by logging into your My Aged Care Online Account.

If you need urgent access to aged care support, call My Aged Care on 1800 200 422 to discuss your specific situation. Some services are available on a short-term basis before or without an assessment.

Find out more about preparing for an assessment and what happens after your assessment .
