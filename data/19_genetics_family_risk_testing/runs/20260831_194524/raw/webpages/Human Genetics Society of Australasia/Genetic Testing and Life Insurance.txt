# Genetic Testing and Life Insurance

## Australia

On 1 April 2026, the Australian Parliament passed the Treasury Laws Amendment (Genetic Testing Protections in Life Insurance and Other Measures) Act 2025 which will make it illegal for insurance companies to use genetic test results to adversely affect an individual's application for life, income protection or trauma insurance.

This ban will come into effect on 8 October 2026.

The HGSA published a Position Statement Genetic Testing and Personal Insurance Products in Australia .

From July 2019 there has been a moratorium in regard to requirements of disclosure of genetic test results in life insurance product applications at a level below $500,000 for death and total and permanent disability, $200,000 for trauma, $4,000 a month for income protection.

The moratorium is now incorporated into the Council of Australian Life Insures (CALI) code of practice, Life Code .

Key features of the moratorium include:

- If you do have a genetic test, your life insurance company must not use your genetic test results (up to the financial limits) unless you choose to declare them.

- Independently overseen by the Life Code Compliance Committee.

People with a favourable genetic test result will continue to be able to use it to show that they do not have the gene pattern associated with an illness that runs in their family.

Life insurers must also take into account any protective treatment an applicant has had (or is following) to prevent them developing an illness that runs in their family.

For further consumer information see the fact sheet from the Centre for Genetics Education - Life insurance products and genetic testing in Australia

## New Zealand

In Aotearoa New Zealand, the Contracts of Insurance Bill was released on 13 September 2024. These amendments have been passed, though no regulations have yet been made and life insurance companies are able to ask applicants to disclose information about the outcome of genetic testing (both predictive and diagnostic).

The group Against Genetic Discrimination Aotearoa (AGenDA) was set up in 2021, and consists of clinicians, academics, ethical and legal experts, patient representatives and representatives from indigenous communities. AGenDA is lobbying for changes to NZ law that provide more protection for people who have had genetic testing who are applying for a life insurance policy.
